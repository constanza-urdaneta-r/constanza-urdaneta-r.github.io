Constanza Urdaneta Romano

Ph.D. Candidate in Marketing · McCombs School of Business, The University of Texas at Austin

Her research examines how firms build and communicate value across the many audiences they answer to — customers, employees, and the market at large. One strand follows the internal life of organizations: how leadership structure, workforce composition, and everyday employee behavior quietly shape the marketing decisions a firm makes, and the outcomes those decisions produce.

A related thread looks outward, toward brand positioning in talent markets, the language and design choices behind branding and packaging, and how firms choose — or choose not to — disclose nutritional information to the people who trust them.

Methodologically, she works across econometrics and causal inference, machine learning, and large language models, using text analysis and topic modeling to bring structure to qualitative material alongside quantitative.

Her curiosity extends past the academy. Constanza has long been drawn to the art market — not only the objects themselves, but art as an asset class, questions of provenance, and the discipline of collection management. Before beginning her doctorate, she worked on the other side of that world, at an auction house and later a gallery.

She holds a BBA and an MS in Marketing from the University of Texas at Austin, with a minor in Arts Management and Administration.
